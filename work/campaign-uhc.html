12 Days of UHC

A twelve-day social countdown for the Self-Care Trailblazer Group, making the case for universal health coverage and the role of self-care within it. I led the strategy and creative direction from PSI's global team, and built the campaign identity the SCTG team wrote and published against.

Dense policy, a scrolling audience

The content covered health economics, policy, gender equity and clinical evidence. The audience was scrolling. One long argument would not be read, and one hero post would not hold attention for two weeks.

A countdown turns a dense argument into a sequence, and a sequence is something people can follow.

The countdown gave the campaign a shape people already know, a reason to come back each day, and a content calendar built into the idea. Each day carried one argument: the gender health gap, the burden of care, self-testing, self-injection, the health workforce.

The identity

A mark that sits beside the parent brand

I built a stacked lockup with its own personality, so the campaign read as an event rather than another post from the coalition. A deep teal disc carries every headline, and a lime ring adds energy at the edges.

The mark sits next to the Self-Care Trailblazer Group logo on every card without being swallowed by it.

The campaign at a glance

One system, twelve days

Each day opened with a numbered cover and carried one argument through a small set of card types. A fixed set of formats meant the project team could write and build each day against a known structure.

Video

Interviews and motion

Four days used video: partner interviews filmed by the SCTG team, and motion pieces built from the campaign cards. I set the format so every video carried the same identity as the cards.

Direction from global, delivery by the project team

I worked from PSI's global team; the campaign was written and run by the SCTG project team. The countdown and the card system were what made that split work. With the structure set, copy could be written to a known format and posts could go out daily, while I kept the strategy and the look consistent across all twelve days.
